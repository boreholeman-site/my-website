Borehole Drilling

How the Drilling Process Works

Drilling a borehole is a detailed process that takes careful planning and the right equipment. Here's exactly what happens on your property from start to finish, explained in plain language.

1
Setup

Rig Setup

Our team arrives and sets up the drilling rig and equipment on your property. This typically takes up to half a day. We'll dig small flow pits alongside the rig to collect water and debris during drilling.

2

Drilling Through the Top Layer

In the Cape Peninsula, the ground surface is made up of sand, clay and loose gravel, which means we start with a specialist technique called Rotary Mud Drilling (RMD). We flush water down the hole as we drill, which carries the debris back up to the surface and prevents the hole from collapsing. A chemical is added to the water to help with this.

If water is found during this stage, we'll case (line) the hole with a strong PVC sleeve. The bottom portion of this sleeve is slotted to allow water to flow in, while keeping sand and clay out. We always drill a bit deeper than where water is first struck, to account for natural changes in water levels over time, especially during dry periods.

3
If rock

Drilling Through Rock

If we hit hard rock before finding water, which is common in many areas, we switch techniques. A steel casing is fitted into the soft upper section, and we then switch to Air Percussion Drilling (APD). This uses compressed air to power a hammer drill that breaks through the rock. The air also carries the crushed rock pieces back up to the surface.

Water found in rock is typically very clean and clear, coming from deep natural fissures or cracks in the formation.

4
Critical

Developing the Hole

Once drilling is complete, we pump high-pressure air into the hole to flush out all the chemical water and sediment left from the drilling process. We continue until the water running out of the hole is completely clear.

Why this step matters: Skipping it, or simply pumping out the mud, significantly reduces how much water your borehole can produce. We never cut corners here.
5

Pump Installation and Handover

Before fitting the pump, we measure the borehole's flow rate and take a water sample for quality testing. This determines the right pump size for your borehole.

A stainless steel submersible pump is then lowered into the hole, suspended a couple of metres from the bottom. The electrical cable and water pipe run back up to the surface and are connected to a control box and valve. The top of the borehole is sealed with cement to prevent any surface water or contaminants from getting in.

If your borehole is on a lawn, we can fit a flush, green fibreglass cover over the top so it blends in and is protected from damage.

Maintenance

Why Borehole Maintenance Matters

Because a borehole is underground, it's easy to forget about it until something goes wrong. Regular servicing keeps your system running efficiently and prevents small issues from turning into expensive repairs.

What We Do During a Service Visit

Everything Checked, Top to Bottom

⬆️

Pull and Inspect the Pump

We remove the pump from the hole, inspect it for wear and check that it's operating correctly.

💨

Blow the Hole Clean

Using a compressor, we clear out sediment that builds up at the bottom of the borehole. Sediment that reaches the pump level will damage it.

🔍

Clean the Screens

Iron deposits gradually build up on the slotted PVC screens inside the borehole. Left too long, this blocks water flow and reduces your yield. We clean these during every service.

📊

Check Flow Rate and Water Quality

We measure how much water the borehole is producing and check the quality. If anything looks unusual, like cloudy water, we investigate and resolve it before it causes damage.

🤏

Inspect Tanks and Pipework

We check that storage tanks are clean and free of sediment, and inspect all pipes and fittings for leaks or wear.

📅

Recommended Frequency

A full service at least once every 12 months keeps your borehole in good health and protects your investment in the pump and storage system.
